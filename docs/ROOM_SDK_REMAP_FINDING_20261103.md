# RGB distortion mapping discrepancy and candidate

Date: 2026-11-03. **Finding: the existing inverse-Brown color remapping does not numerically agree with the librealsense SDK's inverse-Brown deprojection.** This is a concrete convention/implementation discrepancy, not yet proof that every previous pose or depth point is wrong. A separate SDK-consistent RGB candidate was created; the baseline has not been overwritten.

## Reproducible numerical check

Installed `pyrealsense2` 2.59.1.11053 into `.cache/room_sdk_audit/install`, an isolated target, not the project's virtual environment site-packages. The downloaded wheel SHA-256 is `045dfae020a635ba0c2c27e79ea3fa844acb86101344f68ca4c4163beb94b721`. No physical camera was connected. `scripts/audit-room-realsense-sdk.py` used the existing recording's RGB/depth intrinsics, declared `Inverse Brown Conrady` RGB model, and recorded transform.

Across 63 raw RGB pixel positions, SDK inverse-Brown deprojection and the current `clean.brown` interpretation differ by median 11.86 and maximum 19.38 pixels in **raw 1280-wide RGB coordinates**. Numerically inverting that Brown function instead agrees with SDK deprojection within 0.029 raw pixels on those samples. On 35 ideal-ray samples, applying the existing inverse-Brown remap and SDK deprojection misses the target by a maximum 19.39 raw pixels; the alternate forward-Brown remap followed by SDK deprojection misses by at most 0.026 raw pixels. SDK depth deprojection and recorded SDK extrinsics numerically agree with the baseline formulas to within 0.0000004 m in the sampled cases. [SDK report](../results/room_improve_sdk_calibration_v03_20261103/report.json).

This tests agreement with the SDK implementation and the stored model declaration. It does not independently verify calibration accuracy, frame-direction semantics of the recorded transform, RGB/depth exposure timing, or correctness for every point. The current RGB pipeline's behavior on this recording is pixel-for-pixel reproducible, and the difference is not explained by a failed reproduction check.

## Candidate comparison on recorded views

`scripts/compare-room-sdk-remap.py` read ten original RGB messages read-only and produced the baseline and alternate remaps using the same output K and dimensions. The baseline remaps reproduce the saved prepared images **exactly** on all ten. Four additional nearby views were evaluated by `scripts/compare-room-sdk-remap-extra.py`; their baseline reproduction was also exact. The alternate remap uses `brown(ideal)` as output-to-input raw RGB map rather than `invert_brown(ideal)`, agreeing closely with the SDK round trip. Across the entire output image, the two maps differ by median 9.48 and maximum 19.52 **raw RGB pixels**; at this output scale the nominal equivalent is about 6.3 and 12.9 prepared-image pixels. The candidate map remains inside valid raw-image interpolation bounds for all output pixels on this calibration.

The first five development and five check frames were selected before generating candidate remaps; they depict nearby scenes and are not independent scenes. Four additional check frames were selected **after** inspecting the initial results. Depth overlays: [initial comparisons](../results/room_improve_sdk_remap_comparison_20261103/), [additional nearby comparisons](../results/room_improve_sdk_remap_extra_20261103/), [enlarged crops](../results/room_improve_sdk_crop_review_20261103/).

`scripts/measure-room-sdk-edges.py` examines the same projected depth transition against both remaps, on six rows each of three kiosk and three box views. The strips and rows were selected after inspecting both image versions. For the kiosk, median distance from the strongest RGB gradient to the 10–90% observed depth band was 5, 5 and 7 prepared pixels for the old mapping and 0, 0 and 0 for the alternate mapping. These are **distances to a band**, not exact physical edge errors; several bands contain missing pixels and rows are correlated. Seven kiosk rows across the three views have no holes in their selected bands: old distances were 4, 5, 6, 7, 9, 7 and 0 px; candidate distances were all zero. Marker review supports the kiosk pedestal as a plausible foreground/background target, but selection is retrospective and does not establish a global accuracy pass.

Box results are **not accepted as physical validation**: the strongest-gradient markers can land on its label or face instead of the outer edge, and many depth bands contain holes. The existing nearby-view checks are not blind and do not resolve timing or extrinsics. [Measurements](../results/room_improve_sdk_edge_measurements_20261103/report.json), [annotated kiosk examples](../results/room_improve_sdk_edge_measurements_20261103/kiosk_000003.png).

A further retrospective comparison tested post and figure strips in frames 191/195 and 239/243. In frame 239, the standing figure's left silhouette has five no-hole bands with median RGB-to-band distances of 8 px for the old mapping and 1 px for the candidate. The related frame 243 yields only one valid band, so it is weak follow-up evidence. The 191/195 post strips include multiple physical structures; marker inspection indicates that strongest RGB gradients and depth bands can refer to different edges, so those results are rejected as calibration evidence. [Additional measurements and markers](../results/room_improve_sdk_extra_edges_20261103/). These two objects and correlated nearby views do not justify a global pass.

As a counter-check independent of depth, `scripts/audit-room-sdk-straight-lines.py` measured apparent curvature of retrospectively selected vertical architectural boundaries. In map-panel frames 001/002, the candidate's fitted-line residuals were slightly higher than the old mapping (medians 1.18 vs 0.98 px and 0.43 vs 0.30 px); frame 003 was similar (0.61 vs 0.68 px). Backdrop-edge results were mixed and markers show switches between the pillar and other image structures. Some map-panel rows pick image texture and these frames are nearby, so this is not independent proof either mapping is physically correct. It is contrary or inconclusive visual evidence that must be retained rather than claiming every scene check improves. [Line markers and results](../results/room_improve_sdk_straight_lines_20261103/).

## Isolated RGB candidate and impact

`scripts/prepare-room-sdk-rectified-candidate.py` created `data/room_improve_sdk_rectified_20261103/images/`, containing all **477** selected source RGB images with the alternate remap. Original RGB/depth IDs and timestamps, exact 60 held-out identities, per-image SHA-256, and map settings are in [candidate_manifest.json](../data/room_improve_sdk_rectified_20261103/candidate_manifest.json). The raw DB3 was opened read-only; its size and modification time did not change. All 477 images decoded, and ten independently generated candidate comparison images matched the saved candidates exactly. Existing calibration K and selected frame identities were copied for candidate-only RGB pose reconstruction.

**Important:** the new images have changed geometry. The existing RGB-derived camera poses, relative pose graph, original depth/point-cloud audit labels, experimental point cloud, held-out render poses and trained 120k model cannot simply be treated as validated for the new images. RGB/depth alignment, a fresh pose reconstruction, coordinate frame alignment and point-cloud screening must be checked on the candidate. The candidate is not a trained model or an accepted cleaned cloud. The existing baseline and all prior artifacts remain preserved.

The RGB-only COLMAP reconstruction completed with a largest component of **301/477** views, 15,145 points and mean in-sample reprojection error 0.610 px. The baseline RGB-only largest component also had 301 views, 15,092 points and in-sample mean error 0.741 px. These are not matched independent error measurements: detected features, reconstructions and models differ. The run's command wrapper returned exit code 1 because PowerShell treated COLMAP stderr logging as a native error; `pipeline_status.json`, `reconstruction_report.json`, and the output model independently show the Python stage completed. Log: `results/room_improve_sdk_reconstruction_20261103.log`.

The separate candidate RGB-D recovery initially connected 345/477 views. `scripts/bridge-room-sdk-candidate.py` ran the existing intermediate-frame bridge with the candidate RGB remapping supplied only within that process; it accepted 44 of 44 attempted boundaries and produced a connected **477/477**-view graph and 180,000 initialization points. Pre-bridge graph, poses, report and sparse model were preserved under `results/room_improve_sdk_rgbd_prebridge_snapshot_20261103/`. A first snapshot command accidentally copied three files to a single non-directory destination; the resulting extraneous file was left intact, and a correctly named directory was then created and checked. Connection is not accuracy, and accepted bridge edges are derived from the same recording and assumptions. Candidate graph and cloud are experimental.

The first candidate dataset validation failed because our explicit split file used `test` where the loader requires `val`. The failed split files were retained as `experiment_split.before_val_fix.json`; `scripts/fix-room-sdk-split.py` renamed the key without altering identities. Revalidation succeeded: 477 registered, 417 train and 60 validation images, finite samples and positive depth. The exact validation identities match the original source-ID list. Reconstruction and graph optimization use all images to estimate poses, so the 60-frame split applies to later model training, not independent pose estimation. The structural validation does not prove alignment or trajectory accuracy.

## Pose and cloud checks

The two 477-view metric trajectories were rigidly aligned using a RANSAC fit on training camera centers. Among the original 60 held-out identities, the median camera-center difference after this fit is **0.143 m**, with a 90th percentile of **0.712 m**; orientation differences have median 2.63 and 90th percentile 4.47 degrees. These are differences between estimates, not errors against ground truth, and both pose graphs saw these frames. [Per-frame trajectory comparison](../results/room_improve_sdk_pose_comparison_20261103/).

`scripts/compare-room-sdk-paired-epipolar.py` compares both graphs on the **same** SDK-candidate RGB feature correspondences, mapping their coordinates continuously to the old rectification and splitting features before fitting an exploratory fundamental matrix. Nine of 13 chosen pairs had sufficient matches. Several candidate median residuals were lower, some were similar or higher; exhibit pairs are heavily planar. Check features were not excluded from graph estimation, and the F-consistent subset is selected by a candidate-image fit, so this is not an unbiased global accuracy test. [Results and match images](../results/room_improve_sdk_paired_epipolar_20261103/).

`scripts/audit-room-sdk-3d-revisits.py` then checked six of these frame pairs, none of which is a direct edge in either graph. It compared stable, unfilled 3x3 depth at matching RGB features after a feature split. Three pairs had no usable jointly stable depth correspondences; 191/204 had only four and showed median 0.337 m old versus 0.434 m candidate, too few and spatially narrow for a conclusion. For 204/249 and 205/250, 42 and 43 usable pairs were concentrated on the near-planar exhibit: median 3D separation was 0.064 vs 0.065 m and 0.046 vs 0.046 m respectively. This check does **not** demonstrate a candidate improvement. The frames and related observations still influenced the graph, the candidate fundamental matrix selected test matches, and static/physical correspondence is unverified. [3D revisit report](../results/room_improve_sdk_3d_revisits_20261103/report.json).

Built a separate **180,000-point** candidate sample from 387 training-source frames in the candidate graph and checked it against 30 disjoint source frames more than five seconds from each point's own source. At stable 3x3 projected-depth neighborhoods, 48,844 points had no check observation, 45,594 had at least one support, 1,198 had at least three free-space conflicts and no support, and 268 had both at least three conflicts and support. These are overlapping evidence classes, not accepted deletion labels. The check frames still share the optimized pose graph and calibration. [Candidate cloud and uncertainty colors](../results/room_improve_sdk_source_cloud_20261103/).

The candidate's 387 source names, 30 check names and 60 original held-out names were confirmed disjoint. A 200-point sample reprojected numerically back to its own source pixels and depths, and both PLY files reloaded with the expected 180,000 points; this checks source identity, not physical accuracy. The first attempt to run the shared verification script looked for the previous cloud's uncertainty filename and failed; the wrapper was corrected to use this candidate's dense-uncertainty filename and the verification passed. [Verification](../results/room_improve_sdk_source_cloud_20261103/verification.json), [code and artifact hashes](../results/room_improve_sdk_source_cloud_20261103/sdk_candidate_provenance.json).

To reduce point-selection effects, `scripts/compare-room-sdk-paired-depth.py` matched **67,769 identical source-depth pixels** present in the old and new sampled clouds. In that matched subset, the old graph yielded 14,145 points with at least one stable check support and 602 with three or more conflicts and no support; the candidate yielded 15,629 and 476 respectively. No-observation counts were 20,095 and 19,083. The poses change the check projections and visibility, the voxel-matched subset is selected, and both graphs share input/check frames. Therefore these numbers are encouraging diagnostics, not unbiased proof of improved geometry or calibration. [Paired report](../results/room_improve_sdk_paired_depth_20261103/report.json).

Review [captured-view comparisons](../results/room_improve_sdk_cloud_views_20261103/captured_contact_sheet.jpg) using each graph's own pose, and [fixed-world comparisons](../results/room_improve_sdk_cloud_views_20261103/fixed_world_contact_sheet.jpg) using the approximate rigid trajectory alignment. Fixed-world agreement is limited because the trajectories differ non-rigidly; the images use display-only 3x3 point dilation. Neither proves that full room surfaces or thin details are correct.

Mapping, preparation, comparison and recovery wrapper scripts compiled. The SDK check, synthetic tests, per-frame reproduction, candidate-image decoding and edge markers support investigating this correction, but none establishes a complete alignment or geometry pass. The earlier numerical bookkeeping report must be read together with this discovery: matching stored K and transform values did not validate RGB undistortion. No model training or conflict-based point deletion was performed.
