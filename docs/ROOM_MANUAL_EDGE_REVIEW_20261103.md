# Manual review of RGB-depth edge candidates

Date: 2026-11-03. **Result: inconclusive; no calibration or timing correction is supported.** The standing statue supplies a few plausible edge candidates, but other inspected structures have ambiguous depth transitions. This is not a passed alignment gate.

## What was reviewed

Inspected enlarged grids of prepared RGB and projected, unfilled depth jumps for the standing statue, a separate seated statue, the right and left railing posts, a display backdrop and a horizontal region near the front of the exhibit. Images, marked examples and [reviewed candidates](../results/room_improve_manual_edges_20261103/reviewed_candidates.csv) are saved under `results/room_improve_manual_edges_20261103/`. The enlarged grids display RGB at left and valid adjacent-pixel depth jumps in magenta at right; yellow grid labels are image pixel coordinates. The six original audit views and 14 nearby selected frames used for this work were projected from the original paired depth messages.

For reproducible examples, `scripts/record-room-manual-edge-review.py` records specific candidate coordinates chosen after visual review and inspection of Sobel responses and thresholded adjacent-pixel depth differences. Cyan marks the selected RGB location and magenta the first pixel of the selected depth pair. Identical locations overlap visually. This is manual review assisted by automated edge responses, not independently annotated ground truth. Depth transitions can occupy several pixels, so the chosen discontinuity is not necessarily the true boundary. A depth discontinuity's true location falls between its two sampled pixels; its start-point convention alone introduces approximately half a pixel of uncertainty, in addition to blur and sensor effects.

The nearby head-top values have a different orientation from the previous horizontal scanline test, but remain on the **same** head and same local trajectory. They do not demonstrate accuracy at multiple independent distances or establish a global x/y correction. The available far-room views also lack depth on many target structures, and reflective cases are unsuitable as simple static depth controls. Angular motion, viewpoint and depth vary together; no timing-offset conclusion follows.

## Decision and next action

Do not adjust calibration or interpret the rejected post offsets as a verified sensor displacement. To continue, search additional recorded frames for at least two clear non-reflective objects at different distances with sharp RGB silhouettes and valid depth on both sides; collect matched views at different motion rates and mark the same physical boundaries. Reserve distinct frames and structures for testing any candidate. If the recording cannot provide such observations, explicitly scope depth use to regions that can be validated and keep the remaining alignment gate unresolved. The present data do not justify changing the original graph, cloud or model.
